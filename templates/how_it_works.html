How the rankings work

Power rankings try to answer “who is actually the best team right now?” A record alone can't do that, because a good team can go 1–2 by drawing the week's top scorers. So each team gets a score that blends four signals, and the highest score is #1.

When a week counts

A week is ranked once its games are done and every team has points on the board. While a week is still being played, its page shows live scores and awards marked in progress, and the rankings table keeps showing the previous week's order until the week finishes.

The site rebuilds itself every Tuesday morning after Monday Night Football, with a second run Tuesday afternoon as a backstop. Every rebuild re-checks the whole season's scores and roster moves, so late stat corrections and waiver claims are picked up.

Pre-season rankings

Before Week 1 there's nothing to rank on, so the Preseason page carries over last season's final power rankings, matched to each roster. A team that changed owners is marked new owner, the reigning champion gets a trophy, and any roster with no history from last season starts at the bottom.

Position rankings

Each week page also ranks every team at {% for p in positions %}{{ p }}{% if loop.revindex == 2 %} and {% elif not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}: the starters' points at that position, ranked 1–{{ num_teams }} across the league. Bench points don't count. Starters count at their real position whatever slot they filled, so a FLEX receiver counts as WR and a team starting two QBs in the superflex gets both in its QB total. Teams on the same total share a rank. The week's best at each position is shown in green and the worst in red.

Playoff odds

The Season page plays out the rest of the regular season {{ "{:,}".format(playoff_sims) }} times and counts how often each team makes the playoffs{% if playoff_byes %}, earns a first-round bye,{% endif %} or finishes as the #1 seed. Every remaining game on the real schedule is simulated{% if league_average_match %}, plus each week's game against the league median{% endif %}. Standings go by wins, then total points{% if playoff_teams %}, and the top {{ playoff_teams }} make it{% if playoff_byes %}, with the top {{ playoff_byes }} getting byes{% endif %}{% endif %}. In a season with divisions, division winners are seeded ahead of the wild cards, which is how this league's 2022–2024 brackets were drawn.

Each team's weekly score is modeled from its results so far, and the model is careful not to over-read a hot start. Week-to-week swings are measured from how much teams' own scores bounce around. Only the part of a team's gap from the league average that luck can't explain is treated as real, which is most of it by November but much less in September. Each simulated season also re-rolls how good every team really is before playing it out, so early-season odds stay suitably unsure.

At the extremes the odds read “<0.1%” or “>99.9%”: zero hits in {{ "{:,}".format(playoff_sims) }} tries means very unlikely, not mathematically eliminated.

Lineup efficiency

For every finished week, the site works out the best lineup each team could have set from its own roster. Every starting slot, flex spots included, gets the highest-scoring player who's eligible for it. It then compares that with the lineup the manager actually played. Missed points is the difference, and efficiency is points scored divided by the best possible. Players on IR or the taxi squad aren't counted, since they couldn't have started, and eligibility goes by each player's listed position.

A benched player who scored exactly as much as the starter he'd have replaced isn't counted as a mistake, so two players both on bye never show up as a bad call. The week's biggest miss wins the Costliest lineup call award.

Pickups and trades

Every waiver claim, free-agent pickup, and traded player is credited with the points he has scored in his new team's starting lineup since the week he arrived. Bench points don't count, because they never counted toward a score. If a player changes teams again, each team gets credit only for its own stretch with him. Players a team drafted aren't graded here; that's what the draft grades are for.

Trades compare each side's total. Draft picks that changed hands are listed but can't be scored until they're used.

Trade calculator

The Trades page gives every rostered player a value: how many more points per week he should score from here on than a replacement-level starter at his position.

Projection

A player's projection blends what he has actually scored this season, in this league's scoring, with what players ranked like him in Sleeper's player rankings score at his position. That expectation comes from this league's own results: a line fitted through every player's points per game against his Sleeper rank, one position at a time. It counts as {{ prior_games }} games of evidence:

projection = (games × points per game + {{ prior_games }} × expected from rank) ÷ (games + {{ prior_games }})

Early in the season the ranking carries most of the weight, so a hot start moves a player up without making him a star. By November his own scoring counts for most of it. A week he scored exactly 0 is treated as a week he didn't play, because Sleeper doesn't say who was active.

Replacement level and value

To find replacement level, every team's starting lineup is filled from all the league's rosters, best projections first: the regular slots, then the flex spots.{% if superflex %} Superflex spots mostly go to quarterbacks, which is why QBs are worth so much here.{% endif %} The best player left over at each position sets its replacement level. Players on IR or the taxi squad sit this out, since they can't start. A player's value is his projection minus that level, and never below 0, because a player who's no better than a replacement isn't worth anything in a trade.

The verdict

The calculator adds up the value each team receives. A gap under {{ fair_gap | fmt2 }} points a week, or under {{ "%.0f" | format(verdict_bands[0] * 100) }}% of the bigger side, is a fair trade. Up to {{ "%.0f" | format(verdict_bands[1] * 100) }}% is a slight edge, up to {{ "%.0f" | format(verdict_bands[2] * 100) }}% favors one side, and anything more is lopsided. When one side is ahead, the calculator suggests players from that team's roster that would even it up.

Value isn't the whole story, because a player is worth more to some teams than others. So the calculator also plays out every remaining week for both teams, with and without the deal, starting the best lineup each team has available that week. Two good players for one great one can lose on value and still help, if one of the two would have sat on the bench anyway. Depth counts too: a backup who covers a starter's bye week is worth something to his team.

Roster spots count as well. A team that takes on more players than it sends and goes over the roster limit drops whoever its lineup would miss least. A team that frees up roster spots fills each one from waivers with a player at replacement level, at whichever position helps its lineup most. Playoff weeks count by each team's own playoff odds, so a contender gets more out of a December stud than a team that's already out of it. The lineup fit line says whether the deal helps or hurts each team's lineup, ignoring changes under {{ fit_gap | fmt2 }} points a week. Draft picks can't be valued.

Rivalries

The Rivalries page keeps every manager's head-to-head record against every other manager, across all seasons on the site, regular season only. A record follows the person's Sleeper account rather than the roster, so a manager who takes over an orphaned team starts from scratch instead of inheriting the old owner's history.{% if league_average_match %} Games against the league median don't count, since beating the median isn't beating anyone.{% endif %} Most one-sided and most even only consider rivalries with at least four meetings, and the longest active streak only counts pairs where both managers are still in the league.

Draft grades

Each auction pick is graded by value against price. A player's value is what this league paid for players ranked around them in Sleeper's player rankings, so it reflects our own market rather than a national one. Paying less than that value is a steal; paying more is an overpay.

A team's grade comes from its total surplus (value minus dollars spent) compared with the rest of the league: well above average earns an A, around average a B, well below a C or D. Keepers are listed but not graded, since their prices come from keeper rules rather than bidding.
